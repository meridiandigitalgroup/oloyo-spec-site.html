The name means cob of the corn.

In Nahuatl, olōyō is the kernel that connects everything to everything else. It is how the kitchen works: start with heirloom corn from Mexican farmers using regenerative practices, and let every dish grow from there.

Chef Olivia López and Jonathan Percival began as Molino Olōyō in 2021, delivering tortillas and tamales to doorsteps. Pop-ups and private dinners followed. In 2026 they opened a restaurant of their own.

  1. Heirloom corn

    Grown by Mexican farmers using regenerative practices.

  2. Nixtamalized in house

    Cooked and steeped in the traditional way, so the corn tastes like corn.

  3. Ground on volcanic stone

    A molino turns the corn into fresh masa every day.

  4. Pressed by hand

    Finished with Sal de Colima and served warm.

Twenty-three seats around an open kitchen.

Olōyō sits in a 101-year-old building on Gaston Avenue. Guests watch the line work from the center of the room, and a server walks you through the menu and the dishes to start with.

Pastry chef Daiana Guzmán, previously of The French Room, Meridian, Petra and the Beast, and Homewood, closes the meal.
